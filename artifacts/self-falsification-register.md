# QNFO Self-Falsification Register

**QNFO Research Collective | 2026-07-19**
**Status: Published | Triggered by huang-2025-quantum-advantage-audit Recommendation 3**

---

## Preamble

On 2026-07-08, QNFO published the "Manifesto for Honest Computation" (§5: The Falsification Pledge), committing to five self-binding principles including pre-registered falsification criteria for all computational claims. This document extends that pledge by applying it **retroactively** to QNFO's five central claims — specifying for each what evidence would constitute disconfirmation.

The Manifesto's Falsification Pledge applies to *forward-looking* claims. This Register applies it to the existing claims that form QNFO's intellectual architecture. Together, they close the asymmetry identified in the "Vast World of Quantum Advantage" full-spectrum audit: QNFO demands falsifiability of institutions; this document demands it of QNFO itself.

---

## Claim 1: The Qubit Delusion (Ontological Thesis)

**Claim:** "The qubit-gate-circuit model is an epistemic failure — a projection of particle ontology onto a relational, field-theoretic reality." (The Qubit Delusion, §1)

**Falsification criterion:** A particle-qubit encoding (superconducting, trapped-ion, or spin-based) performs a commercially useful computation — defined as solving a problem of genuine economic value at lower total energy cost (joules per solution) than any known classical alternative — at commercially relevant scale, independently verified within 12 months of claim, and the result persists for ≥24 months without classical dequantization.

**Self-assessment confidence:** [speculative, cascade EV 13.5% as of 2026-07-15 evidence synthesis]

**Deadline for disconfirmation check:** 2035-12-31

**Status as of 2026-07-19:** No known disconfirmation. No commercially viable quantum computer exists.

## Claim 2: QEC Thermodynamic Envelope (Physical Limits Thesis)

**Claim:** "Quantum error correction multiplies the thermodynamic cost of computation by 10² to 10³, meaning that only exponential algorithmic speedups can overcome the joules-per-solution penalty." (Manifesto, §1.3; Physics of Computation, §2.3–4)

**Falsification criterion:** Any fault-tolerant quantum computer demonstrates: (a) a polynomial-speedup algorithm (≤O(N³)) solving a commercially relevant problem, (b) at lower joules-per-solution than the best classical alternative, (c) at commercially relevant scale (N ≥ 10⁶ for optimization, N ≥ 10³ for simulation), (d) with wall-plug energy measurement, independently verified.

**Self-assessment confidence:** [speculative] — the 10²–10³× overhead estimate is based on surface code at demonstrated error rates. Lower overhead could come from alternative QEC codes (LDPC, quantum LDPC) at error rates below current demonstrated thresholds. Crossover projections are sensitive to gate fidelity improvements.

**Deadline for disconfirmation check:** 2035-12-31

**Status as of 2026-07-19:** No known disconfirmation. No FTQC device exists at commercially relevant scale.

## Claim 3: Portfolio Allocation (Investment Thesis)

**Claim:** "The rational investment response is a diversified portfolio... only 5–10% fault-tolerant quantum." (Manifesto, §1.4)

**Falsification criterion:** Any of: (a) a commercially useful quantum computer demonstrates joules-per-solution advantage over classical alternatives for ≥3 distinct commercially relevant problem classes before 2030, (b) gate-model QC delivers revenue-to-investment ratio ≥0.5 within 5 years, or (c) classical algorithms fail to dequantize ≥3 claimed quantum advantages within 24 months of publication.

**Self-assessment confidence:** [established, pre-registered] — this is a portfolio recommendation based on current evidence. Will be revised as evidence accumulates.

**Deadline for disconfirmation check:** Every 24 months; first: 2028-07-08

**Status as of 2026-07-19:** No known disconfirmation. Portfolio allocation remains 5–10% FTQC.

## Claim 4: Narrative Production Function (Institutional Thesis)

**Claim:** "The $35 billion quantum computing industry has optimized for narrative production, not computational output." (Manifesto, §1.1)

**Falsification criterion:** A systematic survey of quantum computing industry outputs over any 5-year period showing that ≥50% of funded claims result in independently verified, commercially useful computational demonstrations within 48 months of claim publication.

**Self-assessment confidence:** [established] — this is an institutional observation, not a physics claim. Falsified if the ratio of verified-to-claimed advantages exceeds 0.5 over any 5-year measurement window.

**Deadline for disconfirmation check:** 2031-07-08 (5 years from Manifesto publication)

**Status as of 2026-07-19:** Ratio of verified-to-claimed commercially useful quantum advantages: 0/estimated >100 — consistent with the narrative production hypothesis. Awaiting data from systematic survey (Rec 6, huang-2025-quantum-advantage-audit).

---

## Claim 5: Classical Algorithm Catch-Up (Dequantization Thesis)

**Claim:** "Classical algorithms are advancing relentlessly and many claimed quantum advantages exist in a shrinking window." (Manifesto, §1.3)

**Falsification criterion:** Over any 5-year period, ≥3 quantum algorithm claims survive for ≥60 months without classical dequantization or qualification to non-commercial problem classes.

**Self-assessment confidence:** [established, empirically supported] — Tang's 2019/2021 dequantization of quantum recommendation systems, Ronnow 2014 D-Wave benchmarking, and 15+ abelian HSP reductions constitute positive evidence for this claim. Falsified if the dequantization trend reverses.

**Deadline for disconfirmation check:** Every 12 months; first: 2027-07-08

**Status as of 2026-07-19:** ≥80% of exponential quantum speedups traced to abelian HSP. Dequantization trend continues. Consistent with claim.

## Commitment

We, QNFO Research Collective, commit to:

1. Updating this Register at each evidence synthesis checkpoint (minimum: every 12 months)
2. Publicly acknowledging any claim that is disconfirmed by the criteria above
3. Publishing negative results — disconfirmations are contributions to knowledge
4. Citing this Register alongside the Manifesto's Falsification Pledge in all QNFO publications
5. Submitting all disconfirmation evidence to independent verification
